One metric workflow, fewer conversion mistakes

A chemical recommendation is only useful if the input volume is credible and the output matches field practice. Australian pools are normally discussed in litres, while liquid products may be handled in litres or millilitres and dry products in kilograms or grams. PoolFlow keeps those units visible in the service workflow.

The app links the pool volume, current readings and chosen treatment. It can recommend a quantity, but the technician records the quantity actually used. That distinction matters: treatment may be staged, limited by a label, adjusted for product strength or changed after observing the pool.

From test result to traceable dose

1. Confirm volume

Use the best available pool dimensions or known capacity. A poor volume estimate flows directly into the dose estimate.

2. Record readings

Enter the fresh test results and relevant conditions rather than treating the previous visit as current.

3. Review guidance

Consider the suggested treatment with LSI, product concentration, label directions, surface and equipment.

4. Log actual use

Save the amount applied. That is the quantity that should affect inventory, history and chemical cost.

  • Cost attribution to the serviced pool

Not included in a calculation

  • A guarantee that the recorded pool volume is correct
  • Knowledge of every brand’s label or concentration
  • Automatic assessment of damaged equipment or unsafe conditions
  • A replacement for test-kit calibration and correct sampling
  • A substitute for chemical handling training, PPE or the SDS
  • A direction to mix incompatible chemicals or bypass label limits

Treat dose and cost as one record

When the quantity applied is logged against a stocked chemical with a maintained unit cost, PoolFlow can carry that amount into the pool’s chemical spend. This connects water treatment with the commercial reality of the service rather than leaving chemical usage in a notebook and margin in a guess.

The result is gross contribution after recorded chemicals, not complete accounting profit. Labour, fuel, vehicle expenses, insurance, software, tax and overhead still belong in the business accounts. Learn how to use the field measure in pool profitability tracking.
