A field toolkit, not an office system squeezed onto a phone

A sole trader has a different software problem from a pool shop or a ten-vehicle operation. You need quick answers at the waterline, a dependable record of what happened and enough financial visibility to protect the route. You may not need a dispatcher, point of sale or another customer account portal.

PoolFlow is designed around that solo workflow. Add a pool, record the test results, review the LSI and suggested dose, log what you actually used, attach a photo and move to the next stop. The same visit record feeds inventory cost and profitability tracking, reducing duplicate entry after you get home.

What PoolFlow includes

  • Pool and customer records with equipment and service history
  • Water readings, real-time LSI and CYA-aware calculations
  • Metric chemical dosing recommendations and actual-dose logging
  • Chemical inventory costs and low-stock visibility
  • Daily route optimisation and stop ordering
  • Per-pool revenue, chemical cost and gross contribution
  • Service notes, checklists and proof-of-service photos
  • Offline-first records with optional iCloud synchronisation

When PoolFlow isn’t the fit

Choose a broader operations platform if your essential requirement is:

  • Point of sale for a pool shop or retail inventory across locations
  • Central dispatch, technician permissions or fleet-wide live management
  • Built-in quoting, invoicing, card payments or accounting sync
  • A customer portal for bookings, invoices and service history
  • Android as the technician’s primary device
  • Formal commercial-facility compliance workflows

That boundary is intentional. PoolFlow is strongest when one technician wants a focused poolside operating tool without carrying the overhead of an office suite.

A practical solo-tech workflow

  1. 1

    Plan the run

    Select today’s pools and optimise their order. Review the result against the appointments, access windows and supplier stops you know about.

  2. 2

    Test and decide

    Record the readings, check LSI and review dose guidance in metric units. Confirm every dose against the product label and the pool’s actual condition.

  3. 3

    Log what happened

    Record the chemicals actually applied, notes, completed work and a photo. That creates a defensible history for the next visit.

  4. 4

    Review the route

    Use recorded chemical cost and monthly service fee to spot pools that need a price review, a changed service plan or a closer look at the underlying water problem.
